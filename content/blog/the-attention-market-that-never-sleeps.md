# The Internet Attention Market That Never Sleeps

There is a wall. It holds one hundred tiles, arranged like a colosseum. In
the center sits a throne - the biggest tile on the board. Right now,
somebody owns it.

You can take it from them tonight.

## A market, not a list

Every "paid leaderboard" on the internet works the same way: buy a row,
sit still, decay slowly. Nobody competes because nobody can. A rank number
is just a receipt.

BumpOne.lol treats attention the way a market treats anything worth having:
as a contested asset. Profiles buy **active value** with real dollars, and
active value is rank. The board is sorted by it, top to bottom, and it
moves all the time, because the people below you can outbid the people
above you - including you.

That changes what a purchase means. You are not renting a slot. You own a
position on a live board, and every position has a target on it.

## The board has geography

The top 100 isn't a flat table. It's structured like real estate:

- **The King Throne** - a single colossal tile at #1. Center of the board.
- **The Champions** - four anchor tiles on the cardinal points, #2 to #5.
- **The Elite Council** - #6 to #15, the inner ring.
- **The Vanguard** - #16 to #40, enhanced mid-tier tiles.
- **The Contenders** - #41 to #100 on the outer perimeter.

And below the visible hundred? The **Graveyard** - a billboard archive of
every profile that got knocked off the board. Nothing there is deleted.
Fall off, and you can walk back in and reclaim your turf whenever you're
ready.

When a takeover happens, it isn't quiet. It's a bump battle - a broadcast
event that shows who stormed the throne, who got displaced, and by how
much. The displaced get notified. They know exactly which door to knock on.

## Three ways to watch the fight

Attention is legible here, three different ways:

1. **Power** - the authoritative ranking, straight by dollars paid.
2. **Popular** - community-ranked, by emoji reactions.
3. **Trending** - momentum, by climb velocity and recent bump activity.

And transparency is the house rule: every profile shows its **active
value** (your current ranking strength) *and* its **lifetime spend**.
The board doesn't hide the economics. The economics are the story.

## Your stake carries forward

Here's the part that surprises people: you don't repay your full value
every time you climb. Your active value is credit. If you sit at #50 with
$500, and #1 holds $700, you don't pay $700 - you pay the difference plus
the $10 minimum. Your $500 rides with you into first place.

That's what makes it a market instead of an auction. You can challenge the
throne without deep pockets. You just need a bigger claim than the person
who's standing there.
